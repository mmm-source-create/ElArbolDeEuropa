## English

The papal column contains the 76 registered canonical pontiffs in institutional order. Real families, partners, children and house membership are preserved. Gold dashed lines connect only immediately consecutive visible pontiffs; kinship connection searches remain genealogical. Exports identify succession separately from marriage.

Albert receives a documented Portuguese viceroyalty for 1583–1593. His and Isabella’s sovereign Netherlandish titles remain intact. Their dated possessions share Spanish yellow with a distinct dynastic and military dependency pattern, without assigning them to Philip III as his personal governments. Tournai, Oldenzaal from 1605 and Lingen in 1605–1621 receive dated coverage; Cuijk leaves in 1602 and directly Spanish Cambrai receives no archducal dependency pattern. The Leiden cession and Thomas’s study distinguish sovereignty from military and foreign-policy constraints.

Sicily’s recorded mandate now reaches the map’s Trinacrian surfaces, including the 1713 succession. Sardinia follows Savoy from its 1720 exchange; Corsica remains separate. Local control during the Spanish reconquest of Sicily in 1718–1720 remains a specific chronology to research. Hospitaller Malta and Tripoli share the Order of Saint John’s colour only during their recorded periods, preserving the Ottoman conquest and later French occupation.

Teutonic Prussia and the Livonian branch share an early institutional colour through 1409. The 1410 boundary is a map-policy choice based on documented military divergence, not a legal independence date. The branch’s later autonomy, Prussia’s 1525 secularisation, independent bishoprics and Riga remain distinct. Ferrara’s 1531–1597 and Urbino’s 1521–1623 papal relationships preserve local dukes and stop before papal administration (1624 in Urbino, distinct from its legal reversion in 1631). Earlier investiture disputes require further dated research; imperial membership alone creates no dependency hatch.

Eleven sovereign Forelands mandates (individual intervals explicitly marked as inferred from the recorded Tyrolean succession and documented administrative attachment) and Maximilian III’s administration connect existing people to the Alsatian, Breisgau and German routes. The Tyrolean branch has a separate Austrian shade; reunited Habsburg government uses Austria’s colour. Ensisheim’s disputed occupations and subsequent French control retain distinct labels and sources. Missing medieval people and the 1623–1629 partition are not filled with invented individual attributions.

The reproducible territory-name inventory contains 197 candidates, including composite names and explicitly listed Spanish adjectives. These are review candidates, not ownership conclusions. Jogaila’s union supplies the 1393 Polish-Lithuanian colour while retaining Jadwiga and Vytautas. Horde tributaries have darker ochres with a weaker white wash. Brittany uses English brown in its documented Angevin years, without extending the relationship to later alliances. Holstein’s imperial dependency hatch is removed; imperial membership remains available in the Holy Roman Empire layer. Schleswig retains its Danish fief and the dated common ducal colour.
